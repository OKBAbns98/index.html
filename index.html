Urban rooftop farming

Rooftop farming has moved from curiosity to serious urban policy. In several large cities, empty roofs are now planted with vegetables, herbs and even small orchards. Supporters argue that green roofs lower building temperatures in summer, reduce storm-water runoff and bring fresh produce closer to consumers. Critics note that the soil needed is heavy, so many older buildings cannot support it without costly reinforcement. Yields are also modest: most rooftop farms supply only a small share of a city’s vegetables. Even so, researchers say the greatest benefit may be educational, since residents who grow food tend to waste less of it.
